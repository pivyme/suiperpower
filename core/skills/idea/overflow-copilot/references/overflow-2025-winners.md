## Winning patterns

These patterns recur across the 2025 winner pool. Cite them when helping a user position their project.

1. **Load-bearing integration wins, decorative integration loses.** Judges detect when a sponsor SDK is imported but not central to the product. Winners used Walrus, Seal, Pyth, or Nautilus as core infrastructure, not as a checkbox.

2. **Novel primitives beat clones.** ZeroLeaks (ZK + Seal + Walrus), SuiSQL (SQL on Walrus), and PIVY (stealth addresses) all invented something new on Sui rather than porting an existing product from another chain.

3. **Working demo over ambitious scope.** Projects with a live testnet demo that a judge could click through consistently outperformed grander pitches with no working prototype.

4. **Walrus + Seal combination pattern.** Four winners combined Walrus storage with Seal encryption. This pairing (store on Walrus, gate access with Seal) is a proven winning formula for 2026 projects that handle sensitive data.

5. **AI as a load-bearing feature.** 8+ winners used AI, but the ones that placed highest (Suithetic, Magma) made AI central to the product logic, not just a chatbot wrapper.

6. **Developer tools can win.** SuiSQL (Infrastructure 1st) proves that a tool aimed at other developers, not end users, can take a top spot if the problem is real and the demo is convincing.

7. **University teams are competitive.** Multiple student/university teams placed. The hackathon rewards execution and novelty, not team pedigree.

## How to use this data

When a user asks about positioning their Sui Overflow 2026 project:

- Check if their idea category already has a 2025 winner. If yes, they need a clear differentiation angle.
- Check if their tech stack aligns with the high-frequency technologies (Walrus, AI, Seal). These are proven to resonate with judges.
